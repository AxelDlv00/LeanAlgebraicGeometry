\chapter*{About This Blueprint}
\label{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting-about}

This project follows \emph{Tate's thesis in the de Rham Setting} by Justin Hilburn; Sam Raskin (2021). The
retrieved source is archived under
\href{../../../../../references/tates-thesis-de-rham-setting/}{\texttt{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting}} and is indexed in
the workspace reference manifest. The corresponding preprint is
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.11325}{arXiv:2107.11325}.

Over a field of characteristic zero, let $\mathcal Y$ be the moduli space of rank-one de Rham local systems on the punctured disc with a flat section, and let $\mathcal L\mathbb A^1$ denote the algebraic loop space. The construction uses ind-coherent sheaves, $D$-modules on loop spaces, positive-loop descent, and the Beilinson--Drinfeld local class-field-theory action.

This blueprint is an independently worded, source-faithful mathematical
outline of the paper's definitions, constructions, and principal results. It
should be read alongside the original source; the source anchor on each node
points to the complete extracted TeX. Statements are intentionally marked
\notready until a matching Lean declaration and proof have been added.

\section{Main theorem or conjecture}
\label{sec:tates-thesis-de-rham-setting-results}

\begin{theorem}[Tate equivalence in the de Rham setting]
\label{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting-main}
\source{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting}
\uses{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting-setup}
There is a canonical equivalence of DG categories $D^!(\mathcal L\mathbb A^1)\simeq\operatorname{IndCoh}^*(\mathcal Y)$, compatible with the local geometric class-field-theory action.
\notready
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness and module compatibility]
\label{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting-secondary}
\source{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting}
\uses{tates-thesis-de-rham-setting-main}
The equivalence is uniquely characterized as an equivalence of $D^*(\mathcal L\mathbb G_m)$-module categories whose restriction to positive loops agrees with the constructed local functor.

\end{theorem}
