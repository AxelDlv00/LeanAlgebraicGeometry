theorem generatedAt_of_vanishing
    (hledger : ∀ D : X.WeilDivisor, chi k U₀ U₁ D = chi k U₀ U₁ 0 + degK k D)
    (D : X.WeilDivisor) (P : X.PrimeDivisor)
    (hD : Subsingleton (H1Mod k U₀ U₁ D))
    (hDP : Subsingleton (H1Mod k U₀ U₁ (D - pointDivisor P)))
    [Module.Finite k (sectionSub k ⊤ D)]
    [Module.Finite k (localStepTgt k P 1)] :
    GeneratedAt k D P :=
  generatedAt_of_evalMap_surjective k D P
    (evalMap_surjective k U₀ U₁ hledger D P hD hDP)

end GeneratedAt

/-! ## §4. The threshold: how far above the vanishing bound

`generatedAt_of_vanishing` needs vanishing at `D` *and* at `D − P`.  Feeding it the
bounded-vanishing theorem requires both divisors above the threshold `b`, and

`deg_k (D − P) = deg_k D − [κ(P):k]`,

so the generation threshold is `b + [κ(P):k]` — the vanishing bound raised by the
residue degree **at that point**.  Two forms follow.

*Pointwise* (`generatedAt_of_bound`): for each `P` separately, no extra hypothesis.

*Uniform* (`forall_generatedAt_of_bound_of_residueDeg_le`): the same `D` generating at
**every** `P` at once needs a uniform bound `r` on residue degrees, `∀ P, [κ(P):k] ≤ r`.
That is a real hypothesis and not a formality: over a finite field the residue degrees
of closed points are unbounded, so no such `r` exists there, whereas over an
algebraically closed base `r = 1` works.  This is the precise sense in which global
generation is "the bound raised by the maximum residue degree" — and the maximum has to
exist.
-/
