\textit{Source: Hartshorne, II.6, p.~137 (``Divisors on Curves'').}
  Let \(C\) be a smooth proper curve over a field \(k\) and let \(P \in C\) be a
  closed point. The \emph{Weil divisor associated to \(P\)} is the element
  \[
    [P] \;:=\; 1 \cdot P \;\in\; \mathrm{Div}(C)
  \]
  i.e.\ the prime divisor \(P\) with multiplicity one. The assignment is
  well-defined because the closed point \(P\) is automatically a codimension-\(1\)
  integral closed subscheme of the \(1\)-dimensional integral scheme \(C\).
  Conversely, every prime divisor on \(C\) is of this form, so an arbitrary
  divisor on \(C\) is a finite formal \(\mathbb Z\)-linear combination
  \(\sum n_i [P_i]\) of closed points.
  \paragraph{Lean signature scope (iter-174 pin).} The Lean signature
  \texttt{AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.WeilDivisor.ofClosedPoint} accepts a
  scheme \texttt{C : Scheme.\{u\}}, a point \texttt{P : C}, and a
  hypothesis \texttt{(\_hP : IsClosed (\{P\} : Set C))}. It does
  \emph{not} typeclass-pin ``smooth proper curve over \(\bar k\)'' at the
  carrier level, and it does \emph{not} thread the
  one-dimensional-integral-scheme content into the declaration head.
  Instead the iter-174 encoding is \emph{junk-defined} outside the
  codim-\(1\) regime:
  \[
    \texttt{ofClosedPoint}\ P\ \_\!hP\ :=\
      \begin{cases}
        \texttt{Finsupp.single}\ \langle P, h\rangle\ 1 &
          \text{if } h : \texttt{Order.coheight}\ P = 1,\\
        0 & \text{otherwise.}
      \end{cases}
  \]
  On the off-branch --- where \texttt{P} is not a codimension-one point
  (e.g.\ \texttt{P} is the generic point of \texttt{C}, or a closed point
  of codimension \(\geq 2\) on a higher-dimensional scheme) ---
  \texttt{ofClosedPoint} returns the zero divisor \(0 \in \mathrm{Div}(C)\). This
  convention lets the Lean signature avoid threading
  \texttt{[OneDimensional C]} / \texttt{[IsIntegral C]} typeclass payload
  at the declaration head; the well-definedness consumer must instead
  invoke the bridge equation lemmas
  \ref{lem:ofClosedPoint_eq_single} and \ref{lem:ofClosedPoint_eq_zero}
  to expose the two branches at the point of use.
  On the intended regime (one-dimensional integral scheme \(C\), \(P\) a
  closed point), the chapter's mathematical statement
  \([P] := 1 \cdot P \in \mathrm{Div}(C)\) is recovered by combining
  \ref{def:divisor_closed_point} with the chain
  \(\texttt{IsClosed \{P\}} \Rightarrow \texttt{Order.coheight}\ P = 1\)
  that the prover threads inside the consumer (using the
  one-dimensional-integrality content).
