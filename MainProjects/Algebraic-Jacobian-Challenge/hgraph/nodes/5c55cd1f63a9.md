The global Noetherian hypothesis (equivalently, quasi-compactness) is
  essential: it cannot be weakened to merely locally Noetherian. Take the
  affine line with infinitely many doubled origins, obtained by gluing
  countably many copies of \(\mathbb A^1_k\) along the complement of the origin.
  It is integral, locally Noetherian and regular in codimension one, yet the
  coordinate function \(t\) has order one at each of its infinitely many origin
  points, so \(\{Y \mid \ord_Y(t) \ne 0\}\) is infinite. This scheme is not
  quasi-compact; ruling it out is exactly what the Noetherian hypothesis of
  Stacks 02RV achieves.
