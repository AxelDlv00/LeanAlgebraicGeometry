\Cref{def:picardJacobianWitnessOfHasRationalPoint} separates the construction of
  the witness from the question of where the rational point comes from, and the two
  witnesses below are the two answers to that question:
  \cref{def:picardJacobianWitness} takes the point from
  \cref{lem:curve_hypothesis_gap} and \cref{def:picardJacobianWitnessOfIsAlgClosed}
  from \cref{lem:curve_rational_point_algClosed}. Nothing beyond the choice of
  source distinguishes them.

  This bears on the open question of \cref{rem:rational_point_scope}
  asymmetrically, and the asymmetry is worth recording. A theorem for a curve
  \emph{with} a rational point is exactly the statement above, so that formulation
  requires no mathematics beyond the obligations already listed there. The étale
  formulation is not obtained from it: representing
  \(\Pic_{(C/k)\et}\) replaces \cref{def:inst_has_pic_scheme} rather than
  supplying its hypothesis, so it needs a representability theorem for a different
  functor. That one formulation is nearer to hand is not an argument that it is the
  right claim to make.
