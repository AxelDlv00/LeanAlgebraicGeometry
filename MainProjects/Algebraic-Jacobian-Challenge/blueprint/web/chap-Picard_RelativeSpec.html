13 Relative Spec

13.1 Setup and motivation

The Route A construction of \(J = \operatorname{Pic}^0_{C/k}\) as a \(g\)-dimensional abelian variety is organised around the relative Picard functor \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}(T) = \operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T) / \pi _T^* \operatorname{Pic}(T)\) on the category of \(k\)-schemes. Representing \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}\) requires the line-bundle pullback construction on a relative curve \(C \times _k T \to T\), which in turn rests on a relative scheme construction on a quasi-coherent sheaf of \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebras \(\mathcal{A}\), the relative spectrum \(\pi : \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}) \to X\). This chapter packages the construction of \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(-)\) and its three core properties — universal property, affine base, base change — as the foundational A.1.a building block. The relative Picard functor itself (A.1.c), the line-bundle pullback functor on a product (A.1.b), and the identity-component / dimension theory of \(J\) (A.3) live in separate sibling chapters devoted to those constructions.

13.2 Quasi-coherent sheaves of algebras

Definition 581 Quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra
#

Source: [Stacks Project], tag 01LL (situation-relative-spec). Let \(X\) be a scheme. A quasi-coherent sheaf of \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebras is a sheaf \(\mathcal{A}\) of \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebras (i.e. an \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module equipped with \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-linear multiplication and unit morphisms satisfying associativity, commutativity, and the unit law as sheaf morphisms) whose underlying \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-module is quasi-coherent. We write \(\mathrm{QcohAlg}(X)\) for the category of quasi-coherent sheaves of \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebras and \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra morphisms.

13.3 The relative-spectrum scheme

The construction is given affine-locally: on every affine open \(U = \operatorname{Spec}R \subseteq X\) one sets \(\pi ^{-1}(U) := \operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U))\) regarded as an \(R\)-algebra, and the local pieces glue compatibly because \(\mathcal{A}\) is quasi-coherent (the transition isomorphism \(\mathcal{A}(U) \otimes _R S \xrightarrow {\sim } \mathcal{A}(V)\) for an inclusion \(V = \operatorname{Spec}S \subseteq U\) gives an open immersion of the corresponding Specs).

Theorem 582 Relative spectrum exists
#

Uses

  • Definition 581

Source: [Stacks Project], tag 01LQ (lemma-glue-relative-spec); cf. [Hartshorne], II Ex. 5.17(a). Let \(X\) be a scheme and \(\mathcal{A}\) a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra (581). There exists a scheme \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) together with an affine morphism \(\pi : \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}) \to X\) such that

  1. for every affine open \(U \subseteq X\), there is an isomorphism \(i_U : \pi ^{-1}(U) \xrightarrow {\sim } \operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U))\) over \(U\);

  2. for \(U \subseteq U' \subseteq X\) both affine open, the composite \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U)) \xrightarrow {i_U^{-1}} \pi ^{-1}(U) \hookrightarrow \pi ^{-1}(U') \xrightarrow {i_{U'}} \operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U'))\) coincides with the open immersion induced by the restriction \(\mathcal{A}(U') \to \mathcal{A}(U)\).

The pair \((\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}), \pi )\) is unique up to unique isomorphism over \(X\).

Proof ▶

By the open-cover gluing principle for schemes, it suffices to specify affine local pieces \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U))\) for each affine open \(U \subseteq X\) and open-immersion transition data \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U)) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U'))\) for \(U \subseteq U'\) satisfying the cocycle condition. The transition morphisms come from the ring maps \(\mathcal{A}(U') \to \mathcal{A}(U)\); quasi-coherence yields the cartesian square \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U)) = U \times _{U'} \operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U'))\), so each transition is an open immersion. Transitivity (Stacks lemma-transitive-spec) gives the cocycle condition. Uniqueness of the glued scheme is the standard property of the gluing functor.

The structure morphism \(\pi : \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}) \to X\) is the canonical projection of the relative spectrum to its base, obtained as the colimit descent of the affine-local structure morphisms \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U)) \to U\) over the directed affine open cover of \(X\). It is the morphism whose affineness and base-change behaviour are the subject of the universal-property and base-change results below.

Theorem 584 Universal property of the relative spectrum
#

Uses

  • Theorem 582
  • Theorem 585
  • Definition 583

Source: [Stacks Project], tag 01LQ (lemma-spec + section-spec functor description); [Hartshorne], II Ex. 5.17(b). Let \(X\) be a scheme and \(\mathcal{A}\) a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra. Then \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) represents the functor \(F : \operatorname{Sch}^{op} \to \mathrm{Set}\), \(T \mapsto F(T) := \{ (f, \varphi ) : f : T \to X \text{ a morphism},\; \varphi : f^* \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{O}_T \text{ an } \mathcal{O}_T\text{-algebra map}\} .\) Equivalently, for any \(X\)-scheme \(g : T \to X\), there is a natural bijection

\[ \operatorname{Hom}_X(T, \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})) \; \; \cong \; \; \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_X\text{-alg}}(\mathcal{A}, g_* \mathcal{O}_T) \]

(using the adjunction between \(g_*\) and \(g^*\) to convert between \(\mathcal{A} \to g_*\mathcal{O}_T\) and \(g^*\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{O}_T\)). The bijection is natural in \(T\).

Proof ▶

Following Stacks tag 01LQ (proof of lemma-spec): the functor \(F\) is a Zariski sheaf because morphisms of schemes and \(\mathcal{O}\)-algebra maps both glue under open covers. Choose an affine open cover \(X = \bigcup U_i\). The subfunctor \(F_i \subseteq F\) of pairs \((f, \varphi )\) with \(f(T) \subseteq U_i\) is representable by \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U_i))\). Indeed, factoring \(f\) through \(U_i\) identifies \(f^*\mathcal{A}\) with the pullback of \(\mathcal{A}|_{U_i}\), and the affine-base case (585) represents the resulting algebra maps. Each \(F_i \hookrightarrow F\) is an open subfunctor (pull back \(U_i\) along \(f\)). The \(F_i\) jointly cover \(F\) since the \(U_i\) cover \(X\). The gluing of representable open subfunctors produces a representing scheme, which is canonically isomorphic to the glued scheme \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) of 582.

13.4 Affine base

Theorem 585 Relative spectrum over an affine base
#

Uses

  • Definition 581

Source: [Stacks Project], tag 01LO (lemma-spec-affine). Let \(X = \operatorname{Spec}R\) be an affine scheme and let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra. Set \(A := \Gamma (X, \mathcal{A})\), regarded as an \(R\)-algebra. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of \(X\)-schemes

\[ \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}) \; \; \cong \; \; \operatorname{Spec}(A). \]

In particular, on an affine base the relative spectrum reduces to the absolute spectrum of the global sections.

Proof ▶

Write \(X = \operatorname{Spec}R\) and \(A = \Gamma (X, \mathcal{A})\). Quasi-coherence of \(\mathcal{A}\) gives \(\mathcal{A} = \widetilde{A}\) as \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-modules (with the \(R\)-algebra structure on \(A\) inducing the algebra structure on \(\widetilde A\)). The ring map \(R \to A\) gives a morphism \(f_{\mathrm{univ}} : \operatorname{Spec}A \to \operatorname{Spec}R = X\) and a canonical \(\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}A}\)-algebra map \(\varphi _{\mathrm{univ}} : f_{\mathrm{univ}}^* \mathcal{A} = \widetilde{A \otimes _R A} \to \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}A} = \widetilde A\) given by multiplication \(a \otimes a' \mapsto aa'\). We verify \((\operatorname{Spec}A, f_{\mathrm{univ}}, \varphi _{\mathrm{univ}})\) represents \(F\) on the affine base: given a test scheme \(T\) and a pair \((f, \varphi )\), the composite \(A = \Gamma (X, \mathcal{A}) \xrightarrow {f^\sharp } \Gamma (T, f^*\mathcal{A}) \xrightarrow {\varphi } \Gamma (T, \mathcal{O}_T)\) is a ring map \(A \to \Gamma (T, \mathcal{O}_T)\) and hence corresponds to a morphism \(T \to \operatorname{Spec}A\) by the affine universal property (Mathlib Scheme.toSpec_naturality / Scheme.Hom.toSpec). One checks this assignment inverts the universal map \((f, \varphi ) \mapsto a\) defined by \(f = f_{\mathrm{univ}} \circ a\), \(\varphi = a^* \varphi _{\mathrm{univ}}\).

13.5 Base change

Theorem 586 Base change of the relative spectrum
#

Uses

  • Theorem 584

Source: [Stacks Project], tag 01LS (lemma-spec-base-change + lemma-spec-properties). Let \(g : T \to X\) be a morphism of schemes and let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebra. Then \(g^* \mathcal{A}\) is a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_T\)-algebra and there is a canonical isomorphism of \(T\)-schemes

\[ T \times _X \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}) \; \; \cong \; \; \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_T(g^* \mathcal{A}). \]
Proof ▶

By 584, \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) represents the functor \(F\) sending an \(X\)-scheme \(h : S \to X\) to the set of \(\mathcal{O}_S\)-algebra maps \(h^*\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{O}_S\). A morphism \(S \to T \times _X \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) over \(T\) is the same as a pair of an \(X\)-morphism \(S \to \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) together with a \(T\)-factorisation of \(S \to X\), i.e. a morphism \(S \to T\) whose composition with \(g\) equals \(S \to X\). Tracing through, this is a \(T\)-morphism \(f' : S \to T\) together with an \(\mathcal{O}_S\)-algebra map \((f')^*(g^*\mathcal{A}) = f^*\mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{O}_S\). This is precisely \(F'(S)\), the functor for \((T, g^*\mathcal{A})\), so the canonical map \(T \times _X \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}) \to \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_T(g^*\mathcal{A})\) is an isomorphism by Yoneda.

13.7 Functoriality

Theorem 596 Relative Spec functor
#

Uses

  • Theorem 584
  • Theorem 585

Source: [Stacks Project], tag 01LR; [Hartshorne], II Ex. 5.17(b). The construction \(\mathcal{A} \mapsto \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) assembles into a contravariant functor

\[ \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X : \mathrm{QcohAlg}(X)^{op} \longrightarrow \mathrm{AffSch}/X \]

from quasi-coherent \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebras to affine \(X\)-schemes: a morphism of \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-algebras \(\psi : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\) induces, by the universal property (584) applied to the pair \((\pi _{\mathcal{B}}, \psi \circ \pi _{\mathcal{B}}^*) : \pi _{\mathcal{B}}^*\mathcal{A} \to \pi _{\mathcal{B}}^*\mathcal{B} \to \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{B}}\), an \(X\)-morphism \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\psi ) : \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{B}) \to \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\). Functoriality (identity, composition) is direct from the universal property. The pushforward \(\pi _* \mathcal{O}_{\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})} \cong \mathcal{A}\) exhibits \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X\) as an equivalence between \(\mathrm{QcohAlg}(X)^{op}\) and the full subcategory of affine \(X\)-schemes, with inverse \(f \mapsto f_* \mathcal{O}_Y\) (Stacks lemma-spec-properties part (3)).

Proof ▶

Functoriality follows directly from 584: for \(\psi : \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{B}\), the pair \((\pi _{\mathcal{B}} : \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{B}) \to X, \; \pi _{\mathcal{B}}^*(\psi ))\) defines an \(X\)-morphism \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{B}) \to \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\) via the representing universal pair. Identity and composition follow from naturality of the representing bijection in \(\mathcal{A}\). For the equivalence claim, \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X\) is fully faithful because the isomorphism \(\pi _* \mathcal{O}_{\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})} \cong \mathcal{A}\) (locally identifying with \(\Gamma (\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U)),\mathcal{O}) = \mathcal{A}(U)\) via 585) reconstructs \(\mathcal{A}\) from \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A})\). Essential surjectivity onto affine \(X\)-schemes uses the canonical morphism \(X \to \underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\pi _*\mathcal{O}_X)\) of Stacks lemma-canonical-morphism; on an affine \(X\)-scheme \(\pi : Y \to X\), this canonical morphism is an isomorphism (affine-locally it is the identity \(\operatorname{Spec}\Gamma (\pi ^{-1}(U), \mathcal{O}_Y) \to \operatorname{Spec}\Gamma (\pi ^{-1}(U), \mathcal{O}_Y)\)).

13.8 Construction by affine gluing

The relative spectrum is determined by the affine schemes \(\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{A}(U))\) over the affine opens \(U \subseteq X\). Quasi-coherence identifies their restrictions on overlaps, and the resulting cocycle glues both the schemes and their structure morphisms. The universal property shows that this glued object is independent of the chosen affine cover.

13.9 Further properties

Additional hypotheses on \(\mathcal{A}\), such as finite presentation, flatness, or smoothness, impose corresponding finiteness and geometric properties on \(\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}_X(\mathcal{A}) \to X\). The relative Picard construction uses the functoriality and base-change results above together with such properties in its own geometric setting.
