15 The relative Picard functor and its étale sheafification

15.1 Setup and motivation

Fix a base field \(k\) and a smooth proper geometrically integral curve \(C\) over \(k\). As in 14, write \(\pi _T : C \times _k T \to T\) for the projection of a relative curve over a \(k\)-scheme \(T\) and \(\pi _T^* : \operatorname{Pic}(T) \to \operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T)\) for the induced pullback of isomorphism classes of line bundles (602).

The set-valued relative Picard presheaf

\[ \operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}(T) \; \; =\; \; \operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T) \, / \, \pi _T^*\operatorname{Pic}(T) \]

of 604 was set up forgetting the abelian-group structure inherited from tensor product. This chapter restores that structure and then sheafifies. The first half (15.2 and 15.3) verifies that the quotient is canonically an abelian group and the functoriality of 605 respects that group structure; the result is a group-valued presheaf \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k} : (\operatorname{Sch}/k)^{op} \to \mathrm{Ab}\) on the category of \(k\)-schemes. The second half (15.4) takes the étale sheafification \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{(C/k)\mathrm{\acute{e}t}} := (\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k})^\sim \) in the étale topology on \((\operatorname{Sch}/k)^{op}\), and verifies that sheafification preserves the abelian-group target. The étale-sheafified functor is the object represented by the Picard scheme in 25.

15.2 Group structure on the relative Picard quotient

The Picard group \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\) of any scheme is canonically an abelian group under tensor product of line bundles (Stacks tag 01CR; the inverse of \([\mathcal{L}]\) is \([\mathcal{L}^{-1}] = [\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_X}(\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{O}_X)]\)). The pullback map \(\pi _T^* : \operatorname{Pic}(T) \to \operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T)\) of 602 respects this structure: pullback of \(\mathcal{O}_X\)-modules sends \(\mathcal{O}_T\) to \(\mathcal{O}_{C \times _k T}\) and is multiplicative on tensor products.

[Group structure descends to the relative Picard quotient]

Source: [Kleiman], “The Picard scheme”, §2, Defs. df:aPf + df:Pfs (the Picard functors are abelian-group-valued, and the relative one is a quotient of abelian groups; cf. Stacks tag 01CR for the abelian-group structure on \(\operatorname{Pic}\)). Let \(C/k\) be a smooth proper geometrically integral curve and let \(T\) be a \(k\)-scheme. The Picard group \(\operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T)\) is an abelian group under tensor product of line bundles, and the pullback map

\[ \pi _T^* \; \colon \; \operatorname{Pic}(T) \; \longrightarrow \; \operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T) \]

of 602 is a group homomorphism. Its image

\[ H_T \; \; :=\; \; \pi _T^* \operatorname{Pic}(T) \]

is therefore a subgroup of \(\operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T)\), and the quotient set of 604

\[ \operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}(T) \; \; =\; \; \operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T) \, / \, H_T \]

inherits a canonical abelian-group structure under which the quotient map \(\operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T) \twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}(T)\) is a surjective homomorphism with kernel exactly \(H_T\).

Proof ▶

The construction proceeds in four steps over the locally-trivial carrier

\[ \mathrm{OnProduct}(\pi _C, \pi _T) \; =\; \bigl\{ \, M : \text{a module on } C \times _k T \; \bigm |\; M \text{ is locally trivial} \, \bigr\} \]

of 598, on whose isomorphism classes the relative Picard quotient is taken. It is authored in parallel against two inputs being built concurrently: the locally-trivial pullback–tensor comparison isomorphism 684 (consumed in Step 2 to make \(\pi _T^*\) additive) and the tensor inverse 644 (consumed in Step 1 for group closure); both are themselves deferred targets.

Step 1 (abelian group on the carrier). On isomorphism classes of the carrier \(\mathrm{OnProduct}(\pi _C, \pi _T)\), addition is the descent of the scheme-level tensor product of line bundles,

\[ [L] + [L'] \; :=\; [\, L \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_{C \times _k T}} L' \, ], \]

where \(L \otimes L'\) is the locally-trivial descent of the scheme-level tensor product (645); the sum lands again in the carrier because the tensor product of two locally-trivial modules is locally trivial (636). The zero element is the class \([\mathcal{O}_{C \times _k T}]\) of the structure sheaf, the monoidal unit, which is locally trivial. Associativity, commutativity, and left/right unitality of \(+\) descend from the scheme-level coherence isomorphisms — the associator (641), the braiding (643), and the unitors (642) — exactly as for the absolute Picard group on tensor-invertible isomorphism classes (652). The additive inverse is

\[ -[L] \; :=\; [\, L^{\mathrm{inv}} \, ], \]

where \(L^{\mathrm{inv}}\) is the witness supplied by 644: it returns a locally-trivial module together with an isomorphism \(L \otimes L^{\mathrm{inv}} \cong \mathcal{O}_{C \times _k T}\), so the inverse stays inside the loc-triv carrier and the group is closed under negation.

Step 2 (the pullback homomorphism \(\pi _T^*\)). Pullback along the projection \(\pi _T\) gives an additive map

\[ \pi _T^* \; \colon \; \operatorname{Pic}(T) \; \longrightarrow \; \mathrm{OnProduct}(\pi _C, \pi _T), \]

whose underlying assignment of line bundles is 602. It is a homomorphism of abelian groups: it sends the unit to the unit, since \(\pi _T^*\mathcal{O}_T \cong \mathcal{O}_{C \times _k T}\) by the unconditional pullback–unit isomorphism (659); and it is additive, since pullback commutes with tensor product on locally-trivial modules,

\[ \pi _T^*(N \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_T} N') \; \cong \; \pi _T^* N \otimes _{\mathcal{O}_{C \times _k T}} \pi _T^* N', \]

by the locally-trivial comparison isomorphism 684 specialised to the projection \(\pi _T\) on the \(\mathrm{OnProduct}\) carrier. This is the exact analogue, for the geometric pullback, of the additive monoid homomorphism on Picard groups induced by a ring map respecting tensor product, and is modelled on it field-for-field. Write \(H_T := \mathrm{im}(\pi _T^*)\) for its image, a subgroup of the carrier’s isomorphism-class group.

Step 3 (setoid reconciliation). The set-valued quotient of 604 is taken modulo the isomorphism-class equivalence that declares two line bundles related when their classes differ by an element of \(H_T\). This relation coincides with the left-coset relation of the subgroup \(H_T\) inside the abelian group of Steps 1–2: both encode

\[ L \sim L' \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad [\, L^{\mathrm{inv}} \otimes L' \, ] \in H_T . \]

Establishing this equivalence of relations is a concrete isomorphism-class argument over the carrier; it consumes the tensor inverse (Step 1) and the group law, but not the computational content of the comparison isomorphism.

Step 4 (transport). By Step 3 the quotient set underlying \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}(T)\) is in canonical bijection with the group quotient \(\mathrm{OnProduct}(\pi _C, \pi _T) / H_T\), which carries the canonical abelian-group structure of a quotient of an abelian group by a subgroup. Transporting this structure along the bijection of Step 3 endows \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}(T)\) with its abelian-group structure, under which the canonical surjection \(\operatorname{Pic}(C \times _k T) \twoheadrightarrow \operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}(T)\) is a homomorphism with kernel exactly \(H_T\).

The presheaf \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}\) is generally not an étale (or even Zariski) sheaf. The standard obstruction (Kleiman §2, L1292–L1302): for a \(k\)-scheme \(T\) carrying a non-trivial line bundle whose pullback to \(C \times _k T\) becomes non-trivial after an open cover, the Zariski separated-presheaf condition fails. Therefore, to obtain a representable functor in the sense of 25, one must replace \(\operatorname{Pic}^\sharp _{C/k}\) by its associated étale sheaf. Kleiman §2 names this sheaf \(\operatorname{Pic}_{(X/S)\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\) and singles it out as the functor that Kleiman’s main existence theorem in §4 represents.
