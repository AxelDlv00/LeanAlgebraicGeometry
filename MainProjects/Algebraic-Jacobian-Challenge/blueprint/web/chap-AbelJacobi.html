12 The Abel–Jacobi map

Let \(C\) be a smooth proper geometrically irreducible curve over a field \(k\), let \(P \in C(k)\), and let \(\mathrm{Jac}(C)\) be the Albanese variety constructed in Chapter 11. This chapter records the universal morphism and its two defining properties.

12.1 Definition

Definition 576
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 558

Lean declarations

  • AlgebraicGeometry.Jacobian.ofCurve

[Abel–Jacobi map] The Abel–Jacobi map at \(P\) is the universal pointed morphism

\[ \alpha _P \colon C \longrightarrow \mathrm{Jac}(C) \]

supplied by the Albanese property of \(\mathrm{Jac}(C)\).

Remark 577 Picard description
#

Under the identification \(\mathrm{Jac}(C) = \operatorname{Pic}^0_{C/k}\), the morphism is

\[ \alpha _P(Q) = [\mathcal O_C(Q-P)]. \]

Equivalently, it is classified by \(\mathcal O_{C\times C}(\Delta -p_1^*P)\), regarded as a family of degree-zero line bundles over the second factor.

12.2 Pointed property

Lemma 578
#

Lean declarations

  • AlgebraicGeometry.Jacobian.comp_ofCurve

[\(\alpha _P\) sends \(P\) to the identity] The Abel–Jacobi map is pointed:

\[ P \circ \alpha _P = \eta _{\mathrm{Jac}(C)}. \]
Proof ▶

This is the pointed condition in the defining Albanese property of \(\mathrm{Jac}(C)\). In the Picard description it is the equality \([\mathcal O_C(P-P)] = [\mathcal O_C]\).

Remark 579 Restriction of the universal line bundle
#

Restricting \(\mathcal O_{C\times C}(\Delta -p_1^*P)\) along the section \((P,\mathrm{id}_C)\) gives the trivial line bundle on \(C\), which is the identity point of \(\operatorname{Pic}^0_{C/k}\).

[Albanese property of the Jacobian] Let \(A\) be an abelian variety over \(k\). For every morphism \(f \colon C \to A\) satisfying \(P \circ f = \eta _A\), there exists a unique morphism of \(k\)-schemes \(g \colon \mathrm{Jac}(C) \to A\) such that

\[ f = \alpha _P \circ g. \]
Proof ▶

By definition, \(\mathrm{Jac}(C)\) is an Albanese object for \((C,P)\), and \(\alpha _P\) is its universal pointed morphism. Applying the universal factorisation property to \(f\) gives the required \(g\), while the uniqueness clause gives uniqueness.
