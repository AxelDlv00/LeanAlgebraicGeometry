4 Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence for sheaf cohomology with \(k\)-module coefficients

This chapter constructs the Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence for sheaf cohomology with \(k\)-module coefficients and relates Čech acyclicity on affine covers to vanishing of derived cohomology. The groups \(H^n(X,\mathcal F)\) and \(H^{\prime n}(U,\mathcal F)\) are defined in 3.

4.1 Functoriality of cohomology in the open variable

The cohomology group \(H^{\prime n}(X,\mathcal F)\) is contravariantly functorial in the open variable \(X\). Assembling this functoriality yields a presheaf

\[ X \longmapsto H^{\prime n}(X, \mathcal F), \]

and, varying \(\mathcal F\) as well, a bifunctor from sheaves of \(k\)-modules to presheaves of abelian groups. Each fiber also carries the \(k\)-module structure induced by the \(k\)-linear structure on \(\operatorname{Ext}\).

by the standard composition of the Yoneda embedding, the free-\(k\)-module functor, sheafification, and the \(n\)-th \(\operatorname{Ext}\) functor.

Proof ▶

Each morphism of \(C\) induces, by Yoneda and sheafification, a morphism between the corresponding source sheaves. Contravariance of \(\operatorname{Ext}\) in its first variable gives the restriction maps, and functoriality of these constructions gives the stated bifunctor.

4.2 Mayer–Vietoris long exact sequence

A Mayer–Vietoris square for a Grothendieck topology \(J\) on a category \(C\) is a pullback square

\[ \begin{array}{ccc} X_1 & \longrightarrow & X_2 \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ X_3 & \longrightarrow & X_4 \end{array} \]

that is a \(J\)-cover: the pair of morphisms \(X_2 \to X_4\) and \(X_3 \to X_4\) is a covering family, and \(X_1\) is the pullback. For a sheaf \(\mathcal F\), the cohomology groups of the four corners are linked by a long exact sequence whose building blocks are restriction maps, biproduct maps, and a connecting homomorphism.

4.3.1 Serre-finiteness consequence

For a proper geometrically integral curve \(C\) over \(k\), a two-affine cover with affine intersection always exists. Its Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives the following finiteness argument:

  3. Affineness of the pieces collapses \(H^{{\gt}0}\) on those pieces, reducing the LES to a three-term sequence.

  4. Finiteness of each \(H^0\) piece then implies finiteness of \(H^1(C, \mathcal O_C)\).

4.6 Finite-dimensionality consequence

Assume every affine open of \(C\) has a Čech-acyclic cover with Čech-to-derived comparison data. Then 110 gives positive-degree vanishing on affine opens. For a two-affine cover, the Mayer–Vietoris sequence therefore reduces in degrees zero and one to a short exact segment. If the relevant degree-zero terms are finite-dimensional, exactness implies that \(H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)\) is finite-dimensional over \(k\).
