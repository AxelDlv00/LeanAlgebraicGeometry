4 Finite-stage carrier and base-change comparison

Return to the original curve \(C/K\). Fix the separable closure \(\Omega /K\) of Hypothesis H3, put \(C_\Omega =C\times _K\Omega \), and apply Chapter 3 to obtain the exact pair \((J_\Omega ,r_\Omega )\). The finite-stage package is parameterized in the Lean order \(\operatorname {Pic0FiniteStageGluePackage}\ C_\Omega \ K\): its first curve argument is over the separably closed field \(\Omega \), while its ambient field parameter is the original \(K\), with \([\operatorname {Algebra}\ K\ \Omega ]\) and \([\operatorname {Algebra.IsAlgebraic}\ K\ \Omega ]\). Thus every displayed \(P_0.N.1\) is a finite subextension of \(\Omega /K\), not a finite extension of an unspecified field.

The atlas structure first chooses a finite set \(I\) of affine opens \(U_i\subset J_\Omega \) covering \(J_\Omega \). It records compactness of every \(U_i\cap U_j\), a finite affine overlap presentation of that intersection, and finite presentation of each chart ring \(A_i=\Gamma (U_i,\mathcal O)\). Its first simultaneous-model theorem models only this chart-ring family over one finite subextension.

Separately, separatedness of the represented Picard group makes the exact ordered intersection \(U_{ij}:=U_i\cap U_j\) affine. Define

\[ A_i=\Gamma (U_i,\mathcal O),\qquad B_{ij}=\Gamma (U_{ij},\mathcal O). \]

The package index is exactly

\[ \operatorname {Pic0FiniteStageRingIndex} = I\; \sqcup \; (I\times I), \]

with ring \(A_i\) on the left summand and \(B_{ij}\) on the right. Every ring in this combined family is finitely presented over \(\Omega \), and one finite subextension models all of them simultaneously. The two canonical algebra maps \(A_i\to B_{ij}\) and \(A_j\to B_{ij}\) are defined and finitely indexed, but their finite-stage models, transitions, and equations are not conclusions of either ring-model theorem; those belong to the GluePackage below.

Proof ▶

Quasi-compactness gives the finite affine cover, and separatedness of the represented group makes each ordered intersection affine (hence quasi-compact). Local finite presentation gives finite presentation of the selected chart and exact-overlap coordinate rings. Apply Stacks Tag 01ZM to the inverse system of affine field stages: its transition maps are affine, and the schemes involved are quasi-compact and quasi-separated. The first attached producer descends only the chart rings; the second applies the same finite-family theorem to ‘Pic0FiniteStageRingIndex‘ and descends all chart and exact pair-overlap rings. Restriction maps, inverse transitions, and their equations are the separate next package, not outputs of either attachment.

Under only \([\operatorname {Algebra.IsAlgebraic}\ K\ \Omega ]\), there is a package \(P_0\) with nested finite subextensions

\[ K\subseteq P_0.L.1\subseteq P_0.M.1\subseteq E_0:=P_0.N.1\subseteq \Omega . \]

It contains finite-presentation models of every \(A_i\) and canonical \(B_{ij}\), all algebra maps in the restriction and reversed-overlap transition families, proofs that restriction maps remain open immersions, and the descended triple-overlap equations. The package does not contain or produce an \(\operatorname {Algebra.IsSeparable}\ K\ E_0\) or Galois certificate. These data form

\[ P_0.\mathrm{glueData}:\mathrm{Scheme.GlueData} \]

and a glued structure morphism

\[ P_0.\mathrm{gluedMap}:P_0.\mathrm{glueData.glued} \longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}E_0. \]

Set \(J_{E_0}=P_0.\mathrm{gluedOver}:=\mathrm{Over}.mk(P_0.\mathrm{gluedMap})\). Its structure map is locally of finite type and quasi-compact.

Proof ▶

Use Theorem 11 for the finite list of chart and exact-overlap rings. The ‘Pic0FiniteStageRestrictionIndex‘ supplies the two restriction legs for every ordered pair, while the transition and triple indices supply the reversal and cocycle fields. Enlarge to \(P_0.M.1\) so every restriction and transition map descends and every required open-immersion property holds; Stacks Tag 0EUU is the finite-stage open-immersion principle, under its locally-finitely-presented stage-map hypothesis. Enlarge to \(P_0.N.1\) so inverse and triple-cocycle equations hold; Tag 081E is the corresponding isomorphism descent principle, again with its finite-presentation hypothesis. The contravariant spectra of the descended rings and maps satisfy the exact \(\mathrm{Scheme.GlueData}\) fields, whose standard gluing construction gives \(J_{E_0}\). Its finite affine cover proves quasi-compactness, and finite type of the descended chart algebras proves local finite type of its structure map.

For every pair \(i,j\), let \(G_{ij}\) be the \((i,j)\)-object of the base-changed finite-stage \(\mathrm{GlueData}\), and let \(V_{ij}\) be the \((i,j)\)-object of the canonical \(\operatorname {pic0SepClosedAtlasGlueData}(C_\Omega )\). The exact checked comparison is

\[ \gamma _{ij}:G_{ij}\xrightarrow {\sim }V_{ij}. \]

It is the composite of the associativity/flattening isomorphism for the nested tensor products, the comparison with the selected finite affine overlap cover, and the pullback isomorphism induced by the descended restriction maps, ending with

\[ \delta _{ij}:U_{ij}.\operatorname {toScheme}\xrightarrow {\sim }V_{ij}. \]

Thus \(\delta _{ij}^{-1}\gamma _{ij}\), not \(\gamma _{ij}\) alone, identifies the base-changed object with the exact intersection \(U_i\cap U_j\). Under \(\gamma _{ij}\), both projections agree exactly with the left and right atlas restriction maps. The transition maps agree on triple overlaps.

Proof ▶

On coordinate rings, associativity identifies \(\Omega \otimes _{E_0} (E_0\otimes _{P_0.M.1}(P_0.M.1\otimes _{P_0.L.1}A))\) with the original \(\Omega \)-algebra. The package comparison equations identify both restriction homomorphisms and the cyclic transition homomorphism after this flattening. Passing contravariantly to spectra gives the nested-pullback comparison and then the isomorphism to the canonical GlueData object. The separate ‘pic0SepClosedAtlasOverlapIso‘ identifies the exact intersection with that canonical object. The named first- and second-projection theorems, followed by the triple face equation, verify the complete gluing diagram; no appeal to Picard representability occurs in this argument.

The chart comparisons glue to an isomorphism over \(\Omega \)

\[ \beta _{P_0}: J_{E_0}\times _{\operatorname{Spec}E_0}\operatorname{Spec}\Omega \xrightarrow {\sim }J_\Omega . \]

It commutes with the two structure maps to \(\operatorname{Spec}\Omega \). The attached proved assertion is precisely this carrier isomorphism and its structure-map equation. Transporting \(r_\Omega \) along \(\beta _{P_0}\) consequently gives

\[ \operatorname {RepresentableBy} \bigl(\operatorname {Pic}^0_{C_\Omega }, J_{E_0}\times _{E_0}\Omega \bigr). \]

This formal consequence is not credited as a finite-stage representation by the attachment. In particular it does not prove \(\operatorname {RepresentableBy} (\operatorname {Pic}^0_{C_{E_0}},J_{E_0})\).

Proof ▶

Theorem 13 identifies the complete base-changed gluing diagram with the finite atlas of \(J_\Omega \). Uniqueness of gluing yields the first global isomorphism; the checked structure-map equation upgrades it to the slice category. The last displayed post-base-change certificate is ordinary transport of an existing representer across that slice isomorphism. Since no Picard class on \(J_{E_0}\) and no natural equivalence over \(E_0\) has yet been constructed, interpreting this result as finite-stage representability would be a logical error.

The source tree also contains the wrappers finiteStageBaseChangeOverIso and pic0RepresentableBy_finiteStageBaseChange. Their defining module is not reachable from the public AlgebraicJacobian root, so they are unverified implementation artifacts here and receive no \leanok credit. Their targets in any case remain the slice comparison and post-base-change representation over \(\Omega \).
