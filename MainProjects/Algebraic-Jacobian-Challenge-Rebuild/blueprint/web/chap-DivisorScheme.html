8 The divisor scheme and the representability of \(\mathrm{Pic}^0\)

Grothendieck’s construction of the Picard scheme does not represent \(\mathrm{Pic}\) directly. It represents first the functor of relative effective divisors, and then presents \(\mathrm{Pic}\) as a quotient of that divisor scheme along the Abel map: the Abel map is a surjection of sheaves whose fibres are linear systems, and a scheme representing \(\mathrm{Pic}\) is obtained by descending along it (986, and Kleiman’s Theorem 4.8 and its proof).

This chapter builds the divisor half of that argument for the curve \(C/k\) of Chapter 1, in the form in which the representability seam 1092 can consume it. The three steps are:

  1. a functor \(\operatorname{Div}^{\, n}_{C/k}\) of degree-\(n\) relative divisors, defined on affine test algebras by local equations plus a finite chart adaptation carrying a colength certificate, and its Zariski-localised form (Sections 8.2–8.3);

  2. an embedding \(\varepsilon \) of that functor into a product of two Grassmannians, sending a divisor \(D\) to the pair of window submodules \(H^0(\mathcal{O}(aF - D)) \subseteq H^0(\mathcal{O}(aF))\) at two pinned exponents \(a\), and a closed subscheme \(\mathbf{Div}\) of that Grassmannian pair carved out by the multiplication condition which the image satisfies (Section 8.4); this is Grothendieck’s embedding of a Quot-type functor into a Grassmannian by a twisted pushforward;

  3. the backward half of representability — every divisor class over an affine test induces a unique morphism to \(\mathbf{Div}\), and the class is recoverable from that morphism (Section 8.5).

The forward half — a universal divisor family on \(\mathbf{Div}\) — is not built here. Section 8.6 states a conditional package for it and isolates the unproved statement (1124). It does not by itself produce the open-immersion certificates or the local-surjectivity instance consumed by 1092; those are separate hypotheses at the representability seam.

The finite two-chart presentation in this chapter is an auxiliary window engine. The divisor functor used in the representability argument instead permits certificates on arbitrary affine-open covers of the relative curve. Thus the fixed chart cover data below is not the definition of that widened functor.

Throughout, \(k\) is a field, \(C\) is a smooth proper geometrically irreducible curve over \(k\) of genus \(g\), \(\pi \colon C \to \mathbb {P}^1_k\) is a finite dominant morphism, and \(F\) is its fibre Weil divisor (644). For a \(k\)-algebra \(R\) we write \(C_R\) for the relative curve (354) and \(V_0^R, V_1^R\) for the two pinned charts of \(C_R\) obtained by pulling back the two standard charts of \(\mathbb {P}^1\) along \(\pi \) (355, 465). We abbreviate \(H_a := H^0(C, \mathcal{O}(aF))\) (525). These pinned charts occur only in the auxiliary window calculations. Kleiman’s Main theorem, which is cited below, asks that the morphism be projective (Zariski locally on the base), flat, and have integral geometric fibres (1124); those hypotheses are not synonyms for smooth, proper, and geometrically irreducible, and require an explicit bridge.

A local-equation system (983) is a presentation of an effective Cartier divisor, not the divisor itself: the same divisor is presented by many systems, differing by refinement of the cover and by unit rescaling of the equations. The relation below identifies exactly those, in one move.

Let \(d\) be a local-equation system on \(X\) adapted to a pointed cover \(\mathcal U\), and let \(\mathcal V \le \mathcal U\) be a refinement (\(\mathcal V(x) \subseteq \mathcal U(x)\) for every \(x\)). Restricting each equation, \(f'_x := f_x|_{\mathcal V(x)}\), is again a local-equation system, and it has the same Picard class: \(\mathrm{picClass}(d|_{\mathcal V}) = \mathrm{picClass}(d)\).

Proof ▶

Restriction of sections is a ring homomorphism and germs are unchanged along it, so each \(f'_x\) is again regular at every point of \(\mathcal V(x)\); restricting the overlap equation \(f_x|_\cap = g_{xy} f_y|_\cap \) of \(d\) (984) to \(\mathcal V(x) \cap \mathcal V(y)\) shows the overlap condition, with transition unit the restriction \(g_{xy}|_\cap \). Thus the unit cocycle of \(d|_{\mathcal V}\) is the restriction of the cocycle of \(d\) along the refinement \(\mathcal V \le \mathcal U\). In the colimit defining \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\) (986) a cocycle and its restriction along a refinement have the same class, by the definition of the colimit.

kleiman-picard Call two local-equation systems \(d_1, d_2\) on \(X\) divisor-equal, written \(d_1 \sim d_2\), if there is a pointed cover \(\mathcal W\) refining both covers such that for every \(x \in X\) the two restricted equations differ by a unit on \(\mathcal W(x)\):

\[ f^{(1)}_x\big|_{\mathcal W(x)} \; =\; u_x \cdot f^{(2)}_x\big|_{\mathcal W(x)}, \qquad u_x \in \mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathcal W(x))^{\times }. \]

This is an equivalence relation, and \(\mathrm{picClass}\) is constant on its classes: \(d_1 \sim d_2\) implies \(\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_1) = \mathcal{O}_{X}(D_2)\) in \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\).

Proof ▶

Reflexivity: take \(\mathcal W = \mathcal U\) and \(u_x = 1\). Symmetry: the same \(\mathcal W\) with \(u_x^{-1}\). Transitivity: given \(d_1 \sim d_2\) on \(\mathcal W\) with units \(u\), and \(d_2 \sim d_3\) on \(\mathcal V\) with units \(v\), take the pointwise intersection \(\mathcal W \wedge \mathcal V\), which refines all three covers. Restricting the two equations to it and multiplying,

\[ f^{(1)}_x = (u_x|)\, f^{(2)}_x = (u_x|)(v_x|)\, f^{(3)}_x , \]

and \((u_x|)(v_x|)\) is a unit, being a product of restrictions of units.

Invariance of the class: restrict both systems to the witnessing cover \(\mathcal W\); by Lemma 1100 neither class moves. On \(\mathcal W\) the first restricted system is the second one rescaled by the unit family \((u_x)_x\), so the two classes agree by Lemma 987.

8.2 The relative divisor functor of the curve

Fix a \(k\)-algebra \(R\). A relative effective divisor on \(C_R/R\) is, locally on \(C_R\), cut out by one equation which is regular on the fibres; a local-equation system on \(C_R\) records exactly that, but on an arbitrary pointed cover, and with regularity asked in the stalks of \(C_R\) rather than on the fibres. For the auxiliary pinned-window presentation, a refinement by finitely many basic opens of the two pinned charts is paired with a certificate asserting that the resulting colength modules are finite projective over \(R\) of constant rank \(n\). The widened divisor carrier used by the representability route is a separate quotient in which each local certificate is carried by an arbitrary affine-open cover of the relative curve. The \(\operatorname{Div}^{\, n}_{\mathrm{Zar}}\) carrier defined below retains the pinned-window certificate and is used only for the auxiliary comparison. In either presentation the certificate is the proposed formal analogue of \(R\)-flatness with fibres of degree \(n\) in Kleiman’s Definition of \(\operatorname{Div}^{\, n}_{X/S}\).

kleiman-picard Let \(d\) be a local-equation system on \(C_R\), adapted to a pointed cover \(\mathcal U\). A finite chart adaptation \(A\) of \(d\) consists of the auxiliary finite chart cover data (1102) with pieces \(P_j\), together with sections \(f_j \in \mathcal{O}_{}(P_j)\) subject to the refinement clause: for every piece index \(j\) and every point \(y \in C_R\) there is a unit \(u \in \mathcal{O}_{}(P_j \cap \mathcal U(y))^{\times }\) with

\[ f_j\big|_{P_j \cap \mathcal U(y)} \; =\; u \cdot d_y\big|_{P_j \cap \mathcal U(y)} . \]

(The quantifier ranges over all \(y\); over an empty overlap the section ring is the zero ring and the clause is vacuous.)

In particular, anchored data suffice to build an adaptation: if for each \(j\) one is given a point \(p_j\) with \(P_j \subseteq \mathcal U(p_j)\) and a unit \(w_j\) on \(P_j\) with \(f_j = w_j \cdot d_{p_j}|_{P_j}\), then the refinement clause holds for every \(y\), with unit the product of \(w_j|\) and the restriction of the transition unit \(g_{p_j y}\) of \(d\) (984).

Proof ▶

Only the last assertion needs an argument. Fix \(j\) and \(y\), and restrict everything to \(W := P_j \cap \mathcal U(y)\). Restricting \(f_j = w_j\, d_{p_j}|_{P_j}\) to \(W\) gives \(f_j|_W = (w_j|_W)\, d_{p_j}|_W\). Restricting the defining equation of the transition unit, \(d_{p_j}|_{\mathcal U(p_j) \cap \mathcal U(y)} = g_{p_j y}\, d_y|_{\mathcal U(p_j)\cap \mathcal U(y)}\), along \(W \subseteq \mathcal U(p_j) \cap \mathcal U(y)\) gives \(d_{p_j}|_W = (g_{p_j y}|_W)\, d_y|_W\). Substituting, \(f_j|_W = (w_j|_W)(g_{p_j y}|_W)\, d_y|_W\), and the left factor is a unit on \(W\), a product of restrictions of units.

  1. At a single piece \(P_j\), the chart colength module \(Q_j\) is a finite \(R\)-module if and only if \(\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap P_j\) is closed in \(C_R\) — that is, iff the support does not leak out of the piece.

  2. Consequently, if \(A\) is a certified adaptation of \(d\) in any degree, then both pinned-chart traces \(\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap V_0^R\) and \(\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap V_1^R\) are closed in \(C_R\).

Say the leak of the piece \(P_j\) is \(\overline{\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap P_j} \setminus P_j\); it is empty exactly when \(\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap P_j\) is closed in \(C_R\) (a set is closed iff it contains its closure, and the trace is closed in the open \(P_j\) always).

(i) The piece \(P_j\) is affine (1102), and \(\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap P_j\) is the closed subscheme \(Z_j := V(f_j) \subseteq P_j\) with coordinate ring \(Q_j\) — a point of \(P_j\) is a support point exactly when \(f_j\) is not a unit there, i.e. when \(f_j\) lies in the corresponding prime.

If \(Q_j\) is a finite \(R\)-module, then \(Z_j \to \operatorname{Spec}R\) is a finite, hence proper, morphism. The structure morphism \(C_R \to \operatorname{Spec}R\) is separated (it is a base change of the proper \(C \to \operatorname{Spec}k\)), so the immersion \(Z_j \to C_R\) over \(\operatorname{Spec}R\) is proper, and the image of a proper morphism is closed. Conversely, if \(\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap P_j\) is closed in \(C_R\), then \(Z_j\) is a closed subscheme of \(C_R\) contained in the affine open \(P_j\); \(C_R \to \operatorname{Spec}R\) is proper, so \(Z_j \to \operatorname{Spec}R\) is proper, and a proper affine morphism is finite. Suppose it had one. By (ii) both traces \(\operatorname {Supp}(d) \cap V_\iota ^R\) are closed in \(C_R\), hence closed in \(\operatorname {Supp}(d)\); each is also open in \(\operatorname {Supp}(d)\), being the intersection with an open. \[ K_1 \; \hookrightarrow \; R \otimes _k H_M \; \xrightarrow {\ \operatorname{id}\otimes \mu _\sigma \ }\; R \otimes _k H_{M+s} \; \twoheadrightarrow \; (R \otimes _k H_{M+s})/K_2 , \qquad \sigma \in H_s , \]

where \((K_1, K_2)\) is the tautological pair on the chart. Its \(R\)-points are therefore the pairs \((K_1, K_2)\) of Grassmannian points of corank \(g\) satisfying \(\mu _\sigma (K_1) \subseteq K_2\) for every \(\sigma \in H_s\). It comes with a closed immersion \(\mathbf{Div} \hookrightarrow \mathrm{Gr}\text{-pair}\); in particular morphisms into \(\mathbf{Div}\) are determined by their composites with that immersion. Placed over \(\operatorname{Spec}k\) by its structure morphism, it is an object \(\mathbf{Div}_{/k}\) of \(\mathrm{Over}(\operatorname{Spec}k)\).

Proof ▶

The description of the \(R\)-points is the standard reading of a determinantal-type carve on a Grassmannian chart: on the chart the tautological submodules are free with an explicit basis, the composite above is an \(R\)-linear map between free modules, and a map between free modules vanishes exactly when all its matrix entries do. Hence a \(k\)-algebra map from the chart ring kills the carve ideal iff the base-changed composite is zero for every \(\sigma \), i.e. iff \(\mu _\sigma (K_1) \subseteq K_2\). The carve ideals on overlapping charts agree because the condition they express is chart-independent, so they glue to a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf and the closed subschemes glue. Determination of morphisms by their composite with the immersion holds because a closed immersion is a monomorphism.

The relation of 1118 to 1114 is the reason for the carve: if \(D\) is an effective divisor then \(\sigma \cdot H^0(\mathcal{O}(MF - D)) \subseteq H^0(\mathcal{O}((M+s)F - D))\) for every \(\sigma \in H^0(\mathcal{O}(sF))\), so \(\varepsilon (D)\) is an \(R\)-point of \(\mathbf{Div}\). The content of the construction is the converse direction, that a point of \(\mathbf{Div}\) determines a divisor; that is the forward map of Section 8.6, which is not built.

Theorem 1141 Openness of the rank-one Picard locus
#

Proof ▶

Definition 1144 The canonical divisor of a rank-one class
#

There is a natural transformation

\[ \operatorname {divisorOfRankOne}\colon \mathrm{PicRankOneOpen}(C)\longrightarrow h_W \]

Proof ▶

On a member \(T_i\) of a rank-one presentation, set \(\mathcal N_i=f_{i*}\mathcal L_i\). The counit \(f_i^*\mathcal N_i\to \mathcal L_i\) is equivalently a canonical section

\[ s_i\in H^0\bigl(C_{T_i}, \mathcal L_i\otimes f_i^*\mathcal N_i^{-1}\bigr). \]

The fibrewise criterion for a relative effective Cartier divisor therefore shows that its zero scheme \(E_i\) is a relative effective Cartier divisor. Its fibre degree is the degree of \(\mathcal L_{i,t}\), namely \(g(C)\); hence \(E_i\to T_i\) is proper and quasi-finite, therefore finite, and its flat fibres have constant length \(g(C)\), so it is finite locally free of that rank.

Effective descent for closed subschemes glues the \(E_i\) uniquely to a divisor \(E\subset C_T\). The same comparison after an arbitrary map \(T'\to T\) proves that \(E\) commutes with base change. Finally,

\[ \mathcal O_{C_T}(E)|_{C_{T_i}} \simeq \mathcal L_i\otimes f_i^*\mathcal N_i^{-1}, \]

so \(\mathcal O(E)\) and \(\mathcal L_i\) differ only by a line bundle pulled back from the base. Their relative Picard classes are equal to \(\lambda \), which also shows that \(E\) is a \(T\)-point of \(W\). Theorem 1145 The two rank-one constructions are inverse

Both sides are invertible, and on each geometric fibre this morphism is the nonzero element represented by the divisor \(E_t\); it is therefore an isomorphism. Theorem 1150 Translated rank-one opens cover over a separably closed field
#

Uses

  • Definition 1148
  • Theorem 163
  • Theorem 662
  • Theorem 583

Suppose \(k\) is separably closed. For every field extension \(K/k\) and every \(\lambda \in \mathrm{pic}^0(C,\operatorname{Spec}K)\), there is an invertible sheaf \(\mathcal M\) of degree \(g(C)\) on \(C\) such that \(\lambda \in U_{\mathcal M}(\operatorname{Spec}K)\).

Proof ▶

Membership in the rank-one locus is etale local, so after a finite separable extension of \(K\) we may represent \(\lambda \) by an invertible sheaf \(\mathcal L\) on the base-changed curve. Fix a positive invertible sheaf \(\mathcal A\) on \(C\). Serre vanishing gives an \(n\) such that \(H^1(\mathcal L\otimes \mathcal A_K^n)=0\), and increasing \(n\) if necessary makes its space of sections nonzero. Write this sheaf as \(\mathcal L_0\).

If \(h^0(\mathcal L_j){\gt}1\), choose a nonzero section \(s_j\). Its zero scheme has finite support. The \(k\)-rational points of \(C\) are infinite and dense by Theorem 163, so there is a point \(P_j\in C(k)\) outside that support. Evaluation at \(P_j\) is then onto, and the exact sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow \mathcal L_j(-P_j)\longrightarrow \mathcal L_j \longrightarrow \mathcal L_j|_{P_j}\longrightarrow 0 \]

shows that \(h^0\) drops by one while \(H^1\) remains zero. Repeating this finite process gives

\[ \mathcal L_r=\mathcal L\otimes \bigl(\mathcal A^n(-P_0-\cdots -P_{r-1})\bigr)_K \]

with \(h^0(\mathcal L_r)=1\) and \(H^1(\mathcal L_r)=0\). Put \(\mathcal M=\mathcal A^n(-P_0-\cdots -P_{r-1})\), which is defined over \(k\). Riemann–Roch gives \(1=\deg (\mathcal L_r)+1-g(C)\). Since \(\lambda \) has degree zero, this says \(\deg \mathcal M=g(C)\). Cohomology and base change now gives a rank-one local presentation of \(\lambda \otimes \mathcal M_K\). The construction was made after an etale extension of \(K\), so etale descent of membership places the original class in \(U_{\mathcal M}(\operatorname{Spec}K)\).

8.7.5 Finite Galois descent and the Jacobian datum

Definition 1152 The separably closed descent package
#

Uses

  • Definition 1038

A separably closed descent package consists of a scheme \(J_s\) representing \(\mathrm{pic}^0(C)\), its universal Picard class, and certificates that \(J_s\) is of finite type, proper, geometrically irreducible, and a group scheme. Theorem 1153 The rank-one representer supplies the descent package
#

Uses

  • Definition 1151
  • Theorem 1137
  • Definition 1127
  • Definition 1152

Over a separably closed field, the representation of Definition 1151 extends to a separably closed descent package.

Proof ▶

The commutative-group law on \(\mathrm{pic}^0(C)\) transfers uniquely across the representation to a group-scheme law on \(J_s\), and the image of the identity morphism is its universal class. The translated rank-one charts show that \(J_s\) is locally of finite type. Its identity point is closed because it is a rational point of a scheme locally of finite type over a field. The diagonal is the inverse image of that point under the difference morphism \(J_s\times J_s\to J_s\), so the group scheme is separated.

Choose a point of \(C\) over the separably closed base and use its \(g(C)\)-fold divisor to translate the degree-\(g(C)\) Abel map to \(\mathrm{pic}^0(C)\). After any algebraically closed extension, Riemann–Roch gives a section of every degree-\(g(C)\) line bundle, so this translated Abel map \(D_g\to J_s\) is surjective. It is proper because \(D_g\) is projective and \(J_s\) is separated. A surjective image of the quasi-compact scheme \(D_g\) makes \(J_s\) quasi-compact; combined with local finite type, this gives finite type. Properness descends through the surjective proper map: after every base change, the image in the base of a closed subset of \(J_s\) is the image of its closed inverse image in \(D_g\), hence is closed. Finally, the geometric fibres of \(D_g\) are irreducible by Theorem 1137, and their surjective images are irreducible. These are exactly the certificates required by the package.

Theorem 1154 Limit and faithfully flat compatibility of \(\mathrm{pic}^0\)
#

Uses

  • Definition 1038

The degree-zero etale Picard functor commutes with filtered unions of field extensions and satisfies effective descent along faithfully flat field extensions, compatibly with its universal classes.

Proof ▶

An invertible sheaf is given on a finite affine cover by finitely many finitely presented rank-one modules, transition isomorphisms, and finitely many cocycle equations. All this data, as well as an isomorphism between two such data, descends to a finite stage of a filtered union. The degree-zero condition is preserved and reflected after field extension because degree is invariant under scalar extension. This proves filtered compatibility. Faithfully flat descent for finitely presented modules descends the local line bundles and their transition maps, while the cocycle equation gives effectivity and uniqueness. Passing to the quotient by line bundles from the base and then to the etale sheafification preserves this descent statement, since both equivalence and effectivity may be checked after a common faithfully flat refinement.

Theorem 1155 A finite Galois stage for the Picard representer
#

Uses

  • Definition 1152
  • Theorem 1153
  • Theorem 1154

Let \(k^s\) be a separable closure of \(k\). The separably closed descent package descends to a finite Galois extension \(L/k\): there are a finite-type proper group scheme \(J_L\) representing \(\mathrm{pic}^0(C_L)\), its universal class, and a semilinear action of \(\operatorname {Gal}(L/k)\) preserving that class and satisfying the cocycle condition. Every orbit of this action is contained in an affine open of \(J_L\).

Proof ▶

Because \(J_{k^s}\) is of finite presentation, it descends to a finite subextension \(L_0/k\). The universal class descends after enlarging \(L_0\), by Theorem 1154. The natural transformation defined by the descended class becomes the given representation after extension to \(k^s\); faithful flat descent for both the Yoneda functor and the Picard sheaf shows that it is already an isomorphism over \(L_0\). Properness, the group operations, and their finitely many axioms also descend after one further finite extension. Enlarge \(L_0\) to a finite Galois extension \(L/k\).

For \(\sigma \in \operatorname {Gal}(L/k)\), both \(\sigma ^*J_L\) and \(J_L\), equipped with their transported universal classes, represent the same functor. Yoneda uniqueness gives a unique semilinear isomorphism between them. The two composites associated to \(\sigma \tau \) carry the universal class to the same element, so uniqueness proves the cocycle equation. A proper finite-type group scheme over a field is projective. Given a finite Galois orbit, choose a closed specialization of each of its finitely many points. For a sufficiently high power of an ample line bundle, restriction of global sections to the resulting finite closed subscheme is surjective. Lift the section that is \(1\) at every member of that subscheme. Its nonvanishing locus contains the chosen closed specializations and therefore the original orbit; as the complement of an ample effective divisor, this locus is affine. This proves the final certificate.

Lemma 1156 Stable affine neighbourhoods for a finite action
#

Let a finite group \(G\) act on a separated scheme \(X\). If every \(G\)-orbit is contained in an affine open, then \(X\) has a \(G\)-stable affine open cover.

Proof ▶

Fix \(x\in X\) and choose an affine open \(V\) containing its whole orbit. Every translate \(gV\) also contains that orbit. Hence \(U_x=\bigcap _{g\in G}gV\) is a \(G\)-stable open containing \(x\). A finite intersection of affine opens in a separated scheme is affine: the intersection of two is the inverse image of the affine \(V\times V'\) under the affine diagonal, and induction handles the finite intersection. The opens \(U_x\), as \(x\) varies, form the required cover.

Theorem 1157 Finite Galois descent of a scheme
#

Uses

  • Lemma 1156

Let \(L/k\) be finite Galois and let an \(L\)-scheme \(X_L\) carry a semilinear Galois action satisfying the cocycle condition. If every orbit lies in an affine open, then there are a \(k\)-scheme \(X\) and an isomorphism \(X\times _kL\simeq X_L\) inducing the given action.

Proof ▶

By Lemma 1156, cover \(X_L\) by stable affine opens \(U_i=\operatorname{Spec}A_i\). Semilinear Galois descent for algebras gives \(B_i=A_i^G\) and an isomorphism \(L\otimes _kB_i\simeq A_i\). Since \(X_L\) is separated, every overlap \(U_i\cap U_j\) and every triple overlap is affine and stable. Applying the same invariant construction to these overlaps descends both open immersions \(U_i\cap U_j\rightrightarrows U_i,U_j\); faithful flatness of \(L/k\) reflects the open immersions and their cocycle identities. The affine schemes \(\operatorname{Spec}B_i\) therefore glue along the descended overlaps to a \(k\)-scheme \(X\). The local isomorphisms \(L\otimes _kB_i\simeq A_i\) agree on overlaps and glue to \(X\times _kL\simeq X_L\). Their defining invariant maps identify the canonical descent action with the prescribed semilinear action.

Theorem 1158 Finite Galois descent of a representation
#

Uses

  • Theorem 1157

Let \(F\) be a set-valued sheaf on \(k\)-schemes satisfying faithfully flat descent, and let \(L/k\) be finite Galois. Suppose \(F_L\) is represented by \(X_L\), and the universal element is preserved by a semilinear Galois action on \(X_L\) satisfying the cocycle condition and the orbit-in-affine hypothesis.
