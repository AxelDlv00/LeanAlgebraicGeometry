3 Curves over a field

Throughout this chapter \(k\) is a field. As in Chapter 1, a smooth curve over \(k\) is a \(k\)-scheme which is smooth of relative dimension one, proper, and geometrically irreducible over \(k\). This chapter provides the geometric substrate for the genus: smooth schemes over a field are reduced, so a smooth curve is geometrically integral; a proper geometrically integral scheme over a field has no global functions besides the constants; the projective line carries two affine charts with polynomial section rings and Laurent-polynomial overlap; the section rings of the standard smooth charts of a curve are Dedekind domains, so the local rings of the curve are valuation rings and its specialization order has height one; a rational function transcendental over \(k\) therefore spreads out to a locally quasi-finite morphism \(C \to \mathbb {P}^1\); and Zariski’s main theorem upgrades it to a finite one.

Lemma 17 Unramified flat algebras over a domain are reduced

  • Algebra.FormallyUnramified.isReduced_of_isDomain

Let \(B\) be an integral domain and let \(S\) be a commutative \(B\)-algebra which is formally unramified, essentially of finite type, and flat over \(B\). Then \(S\) is reduced.

Proof ▶

Let \(K = \operatorname{Frac}B\) be the fraction field. The \(K\)-algebra \(K \otimes _B S\) is again formally unramified and essentially of finite type, both properties being stable under base change, and a formally unramified, essentially of finite type algebra over a field is reduced. It therefore suffices to embed \(S\) into \(K \otimes _B S\). The canonical map \(s \mapsto 1 \otimes s\) is, up to the isomorphism \(S \cong B \otimes _B S\), obtained from the injection \(B \hookrightarrow K\) by tensoring with \(S\); since \(S\) is flat over \(B\), it is injective. A subring of a reduced ring is reduced.

Theorem 21 Smooth schemes over a field are reduced
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • AlgebraicGeometry.Smooth.isReduced_of_field

Let \(K\) be a field and let \(X\) be a scheme which is smooth over \(\operatorname{Spec}K\). Then \(X\) is reduced.

Proof ▶

A smooth morphism is, locally on the source and the target, standard smooth: every point \(x \in X\) has an affine open neighbourhood \(V\), mapping into an affine open \(U\) of the base, such that the induced ring homomorphism \(\Gamma (U) \to \Gamma (V)\) is standard smooth. Since \(\operatorname{Spec}K\) has a single point and \(U\) is a nonempty open subset containing the image of \(x\), we have \(U = \operatorname{Spec}K\), and \(\Gamma (U) \cong K\) is a field, in particular a domain. By 19 the ring \(\Gamma (V)\) is reduced, hence the affine scheme \(V\) is reduced. The opens \(V\) cover \(X\), and a scheme covered by reduced open subschemes is reduced.

3.3 Global sections of a proper geometrically integral scheme

Lemma 31 Pushout of a field extension along itself
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • CommRingCat.epi_of_isPushout_of_isField

Let \(\varphi : A \to B\) and \(\psi : A \to C\) be homomorphisms of commutative rings with \(B\) nontrivial, and let \(D\), with coprojections \(\iota _B : B \to D\) and \(\iota _C : C \to D\), be a pushout of \((\varphi , \psi )\) (for instance \(D = B \otimes _A C\)). Assume that \(D\) is a field and that there is a ring isomorphism \(e : C \cong B\) with \(e \circ \psi = \varphi \). Then \(\varphi \) is an epimorphism in the category of commutative rings.

Proof ▶

Let \(\mu : D \to B\) be the homomorphism with \(\mu \circ \iota _B = \operatorname{id}_B\) and \(\mu \circ \iota _C = e\), given by the universal property of the pushout (the two maps agree on \(A\) because \(e \circ \psi = \varphi \)). Since \(D\) is a field and \(B\) is nontrivial, \(\mu \) is injective; it is surjective because \(\mu \circ \iota _B = \operatorname{id}_B\). Hence \(\mu \) is an isomorphism, and from \(\mu \circ \iota _B = \operatorname{id}_B = \mu \circ (\iota _C \circ e^{-1})\) we conclude \(\iota _B = \iota _C \circ e^{-1}\).

Now let \(u, v : B \to W\) satisfy \(u \circ \varphi = v \circ \varphi \). Then \(u \circ \varphi = (v \circ e) \circ \psi \), so there is \(w : D \to W\) with \(w \circ \iota _B = u\) and \(w \circ \iota _C = v \circ e\). Therefore

\[ u = w \circ \iota _B = w \circ \iota _C \circ e^{-1} = v \circ e \circ e^{-1} = v, \]

which proves that \(\varphi \) is an epimorphism.

Theorem 33 Constant functions on a proper geometrically integral scheme
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • AlgebraicGeometry.bijective_appTop_of_isProper_of_geometricallyIntegral

Let \(K\) be a field and let \(X\) be a scheme, proper and geometrically integral over \(K\). Then the ring homomorphism \(\Gamma (\operatorname{Spec}K, \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}K}) \to \Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_{X})\) induced by the structure morphism is bijective; identifying \(\Gamma (\operatorname{Spec}K, \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}K}) = K\), this says \(\Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_{X}) = K\).

Proof ▶

Write \(A = \Gamma (X, \mathcal{O}_{X})\) and let \(\varphi : K \to A\) be the map in question. Applying geometric integrality to the trivial extension \(K \to K\) shows that \(X\) is integral; in particular \(X\) is nonempty and \(A\) is a nontrivial domain, so \(\varphi \), a ring homomorphism out of a field into a nontrivial ring, is injective. By 32 (properness includes universal closedness), \(A\) is a field, and since \(f\) is proper, \(A\) is moreover a finite \(K\)-module. As a finite ring homomorphism is surjective as soon as it is an epimorphism of commutative rings, it remains to prove that \(\varphi \) is an epimorphism.

Let \(q : \operatorname{Spec}A \to \operatorname{Spec}K\) be the morphism induced by \(\varphi \) and form the base change \(Y = X \times _{\operatorname{Spec}K} \operatorname{Spec}A\). Since \(A\) is a field, geometric integrality of \(f\) makes \(Y\) integral, and the projection \(Y \to \operatorname{Spec}A\) is universally closed as a base change of \(f\); hence \(D := \Gamma (Y, \mathcal{O}_{Y})\) is a field by 32. On the other hand, \(X\) is quasi-compact and quasi-separated as a topological space (28, 29), and \(q\) is flat because every vector space over \(K\) is flat; flat base change for global sections therefore exhibits the square of section rings

\[ \begin{array}{ccc} K & \longrightarrow & A \\ \downarrow & & \downarrow \\ A & \longrightarrow & D \end{array} \]

(left column \(\varphi \), top row \(\varphi \) transported through the canonical isomorphism \(\Gamma (\operatorname{Spec}A, \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}A}) \cong A\)) as a pushout square of commutative rings, \(D \cong A \otimes _K A\). Its pushout \(D\) is a field and its two legs are isomorphic copies of \(\varphi \), so \(\varphi \) is an epimorphism by 31.

3.7 Unramified finite-type algebras over Dedekind domains

The results of this section are pure commutative algebra, stated in full generality. The first theorem strengthens the classical statement that a domain which is unramified and module-finite over a Dedekind domain is Dedekind: module-finiteness is relaxed to finite type. The point of the generalization is where incomparability of primes comes from — for module-finite algebras it follows from integrality (lying over/incomparability), while here it follows from quasi-finiteness, which every unramified algebra essentially of finite type enjoys.

Theorem 76 Unramified finite-type domains over a Dedekind domain, DVR form
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • IsDedekindDomainDvr.of_formallyUnramified_of_finiteType

Let \(A\) be a Dedekind domain and \(B\) an integral domain which is an \(A\)-algebra of finite type and formally unramified over \(A\). Then \(B\) is Noetherian, and the localization of \(B\) at every nonzero prime ideal is a discrete valuation ring.

Proof ▶

\(B\) is Noetherian as a finite-type algebra over the Noetherian ring \(A\) (Hilbert basis theorem). Let \(\mathfrak q\) be a nonzero prime of \(B\) and set \(\mathfrak p = \mathfrak q \cap A\). The localization \(B_{\mathfrak q}\) is a Noetherian local domain and not a field (its maximal ideal is nonzero because \(\mathfrak q\) is), so it suffices to show that its maximal ideal \(\mathfrak q B_{\mathfrak q}\) is principal.

Step 1: \(\mathfrak p \neq 0\). An algebra which is formally unramified and of finite type is quasi-finite, and over a quasi-finite algebra two comparable primes of \(B\) lying over the same prime of \(A\) coincide. If \(\mathfrak p\) were zero, the comparable primes \(0 \subseteq \mathfrak q\) of \(B\) would both contract to \(0\) in \(A\), forcing \(\mathfrak q = 0\), a contradiction. Since \(A\) is Dedekind, the nonzero prime \(\mathfrak p\) is maximal, and \(A_{\mathfrak p}\) is a discrete valuation ring; in particular \(\mathfrak p A_{\mathfrak p}\) is principal.

Step 2: \(\mathfrak q\) is minimal over \(\mathfrak p B\). The fiber ring \(B/\mathfrak p B\) is an algebra over the residue field \(A/\mathfrak p\) which is again formally unramified (base change); every module over a field is free, and an unramified algebra which is free as a module is module-finite. Hence \(B/\mathfrak p B\) is a finite module over the field \(A/\mathfrak p\), in particular an Artinian ring, so all of its primes are minimal; applying this to the image of \(\mathfrak q\) shows that \(\mathfrak q\) is a minimal prime over \(\mathfrak p B\).

Step 3: \(\mathfrak q B_{\mathfrak q} = \mathfrak p B_{\mathfrak q}\). Since \(\mathfrak p\) is maximal and \(B\) is unramified over \(A\), the extension \(\mathfrak p B\) is a radical ideal of \(B\) (the fiber ring \(B/\mathfrak p B\) is reduced, being unramified and essentially of finite type over a field). Localization commutes with radicals, so \(\mathfrak p B_{\mathfrak q}\) is a radical ideal of \(B_{\mathfrak q}\); and because \(\mathfrak q\) is minimal over \(\mathfrak p B\) (Step 2), the radical of \(\mathfrak p B_{\mathfrak q}\) is exactly the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak q B_{\mathfrak q}\). A radical ideal equals its own radical, so \(\mathfrak p B_{\mathfrak q} = \mathfrak q B_{\mathfrak q}\).

Step 4: conclusion. By Step 3 and Step 1, the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak q B_{\mathfrak q}\) is the extension of the principal ideal \(\mathfrak p A_{\mathfrak p} \subseteq A_{\mathfrak p}\) along \(A_{\mathfrak p} \to B_{\mathfrak q}\), hence principal. A Noetherian local domain which is not a field and whose maximal ideal is principal is a discrete valuation ring.

Proof ▶

Write \(\xi \) for the generic point of \(X\) and \(K(X) = \mathcal{O}_{X,\xi }\) for the function field, a field under \(k\) via the structure map of 117.

(a) The generic morphism. By 90 choose \(f_0 \in K(X)\) transcendental over \(k\); in particular \(f_0 \neq 0\) (the polynomial \(T\) does not vanish at \(f_0\)). Let \(\varphi _K = [1 : f_0] : \operatorname{Spec}K(X) \to \mathbb {P}^1\) be the chart-\(0\) morphism of 108. It lies over \(k\), compatibly with the canonical morphism \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X) \to X\), by 109 and 118(1).

(b) Local regularity. Let \(x \in X\). The stalk \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\) is a valuation ring (88) with fraction field \(K(X)\), so \(f_0\) or \(f_0^{-1}\) lies in \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\). In the first case, with \(f_0 = a\) in \(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\), set \(\varphi _x = [1 : a] : \operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{X,x} \to \mathbb {P}^1\) through chart \(0\); in the second, with \(f_0^{-1} = b\), set \(\varphi _x = [b : 1]\) through chart \(1\). (c) Spreading out. The projective line is locally of finite type and separated over \(k\) (40). By 115(1), \(\varphi _K\) is the function-field morphism of a rational \(k\)-map \(F : X \dashrightarrow \mathbb {P}^1\). Every point \(x\) lies in the domain of definition of \(F\): the morphism \(\varphi _x\) of (b) spreads out to a partial map defined at \(x\) (115(2)) whose function-field morphism is the restriction of \(\varphi _x\) to \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X)\) (116), i.e. \(\varphi _K\) — so this partial map represents \(F\). Since \(X\) is reduced and \(\mathbb {P}^1\) separated (106), \(F\) has a largest representative (115(3)), defined on the whole of \(X\): an honest morphism \(\pi : X \to \mathbb {P}^1\) whose restriction to \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X)\) is \(\varphi _K\).

(d) \(\pi \) lies over \(k\). The canonical morphism \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X) \to X\) hits the generic point, hence is dominant, and \(\mathbb {P}^1\) is separated; two morphisms out of the reduced scheme \(X\) to a separated scheme agreeing after composition with a dominant morphism agree. Both \(\pi \) followed by the structure morphism of \(\mathbb {P}^1\) and the structure morphism \(f\) of \(X\) restrict on \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X)\) to the structure morphism of \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X)\) (step (a)), so they agree.

(e) \(\pi \) is proper and locally of finite type. By (d), \(\pi \) followed by the separated structure morphism of \(\mathbb {P}^1\) is the proper, locally of finite type morphism \(f\); properness and local finite-typeness descend along such factorizations (cancellation).

(f) \(\pi \) maps \(\xi \) to the generic point of \(\mathbb {P}^1\). The point \(\pi (\xi )\) is the image of the unique point of \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X)\) under \(\varphi _K\), and \(\varphi _K = [1 : f_0]\) sends it to the generic point of \(\mathbb {P}^1\) by 113 (the hypothesis is the transcendence of \(f_0\), and the unique point of \(\operatorname{Spec}K(X)\) is its generic point).

(g) All fibers of \(\pi \) are finite. The underlying space of \(X\) is Noetherian: \(X\) is locally of finite type over a field, hence locally Noetherian, and quasi-compact (proper). Its specialization order has height one (89).

The fiber of the generic point of \(\mathbb {P}^1\) is contained in \(\{ \xi \} \): suppose \(x \neq \xi \) with \(\pi (x)\) generic. Then \(\{ x\} \) is closed (85), and \(\pi \), being proper, is a closed map, so \(\{ \pi (x)\} \) is closed in \(\mathbb {P}^1\). But the closure of the generic point is all of \(\mathbb {P}^1\), which has a second point (103) — a contradiction.

Any other fiber is finite: for \(y\) a non-generic point of \(\mathbb {P}^1\), the singleton \(\{ y\} \) is closed (105), so the fiber \(\pi ^{-1}(y)\) is closed in \(X\), and it avoids \(\xi \) because \(\pi (\xi )\) is the generic point of \(\mathbb {P}^1\) (step (f)), which differs from \(y\). By 86 the fiber is finite.

(h) A morphism which is locally of finite type and has finite fibers is locally quasi-finite; by (e) and (g), \(\pi \) is locally quasi-finite.

(i) \(\pi \) is dominant. By (f) the image of \(\pi \) contains the generic point of \(\mathbb {P}^1\), whose closure is all of \(\mathbb {P}^1\); so the image of \(\pi \) is dense, i.e. \(\pi \) is dominant.

3.20 Rational points over a separably closed field

Over a separably closed field \(K\) the \(K\)-rational points of a smooth curve are dense: every nonempty open contains one. 3.21 Coheight and the Krull dimension of stalks

For a point \(z\) of a topological space, the coheight of \(z\) is the supremum of the lengths of strictly increasing chains starting at \(z\) in the specialisation preorder (\(y \leq z\) iff \(z\) specialises to \(y\), i.e. \(y \in \overline{\{ z\} }\)); on a scheme it measures the codimension of the closure of \(z\). This section identifies it with the Krull dimension of the stalk.

Lemma 164 Coheight is local on opens
✓

Let \(X\) be a topological space, \(U \subseteq X\) an open subset and \(z \in U\). The coheight of \(z\) computed in \(X\) (for the specialisation preorder) equals the coheight of \(z\) computed in the subspace \(U\).

Proof ▶

(\(\geq \)) The inclusion \(U \to X\) is continuous, hence monotone for the specialisation preorders, and injective; a strictly increasing chain in \(U\) starting at \(z\) therefore maps to one in \(X\) of the same length (strictness is preserved: if the images were comparable both ways they would be equal).

(\(\leq \)) Conversely, let \(z = p_0 {\lt} p_1 {\lt} \dots {\lt} p_n\) be a chain in \(X\) starting at \(z\). Each \(p_i\) is a generisation of \(z\): \(z \in \overline{\{ p_i\} }\). Since \(U\) is open and \(z \in U\), every generisation of a point of \(U\) lies in \(U\) (an open set containing a point of the closure of \(\{ p_i\} \) meets \(\{ p_i\} \)), so all \(p_i \in U\). The specialisation preorder of the subspace \(U\) agrees with that of \(X\) on points of \(U\) when \(U\) is open, so the chain lives in \(U\) with the same strict inequalities and the same length.

3.22 The dimension of a standard-smooth algebra at a maximal ideal

This section proves the dimension half of the smooth-implies-regular pipeline at closed points: for a standard-smooth algebra of relative dimension \(n\) over a field, every maximal ideal has height at least \(n\).

Theorem 168 Maximal ideals of polynomial rings have full height
✓

Let \(k\) be a field, \(\iota \) a finite index type, and \(M\) a maximal ideal of the polynomial ring \(k[x_i : i \in \iota ]\). Then the height of \(M\) is \(\# \iota \).

Proof ▶

Induct on the finite type \(\iota \) (along equivalences, the empty type, and adding one variable). Heights are invariant under ring isomorphisms, which handles transport along an equivalence \(\iota \cong \iota '\). If \(\iota \) is empty the ring is the field \(k\), of Krull dimension \(0\), so every maximal ideal has height \(0 = \# \iota \). 3.23 The trdeg–height inequality and smooth-prime regularity

This section closes the smooth-implies-regular pipeline at arbitrary primes of a standard-smooth algebra over a perfect field, with no recourse to Serre’s homological characterisation of regularity (and hence no need for the theorem that localisations of regular local rings are regular): the cotangent dimension at a prime is computed directly from the conormal sequence, and matched against a transcendence-degree/height inequality proved by induction on the number of variables.

Lemma 172 One-variable step of the trdeg–height inequality
✓

stacks-project Let \(k\) be a field, \(R\) a Noetherian \(k\)-algebra, \(P \subseteq R[X]\) a prime ideal and \(\mathfrak p = P \cap R\). Then one of the following holds:

  1. \(\mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak p) + 1 \leq \mathrm{ht}(P)\) and \(\mathrm{trdeg}_k(R/\mathfrak p) \leq \mathrm{trdeg}_k(R[X]/P)\); or

Write \(A = R/\mathfrak p\), a domain. Case \(Q \neq (0)\). Then \((0) \subsetneq Q\) is a chain of primes in \(A[X]\), so \(\mathrm{ht}(Q) \geq 1\) and \(\mathrm{ht}(P) \geq \mathrm{ht}(\mathfrak p) + 1\). Moreover \(Q\) contracts to \((0)\) in \(A\), so the composite \(A \to A[X] \to A[X]/Q \cong R[X]/P\) is injective, whence \(\mathrm{trdeg}_k(A) \leq \mathrm{trdeg}_k(R[X]/P)\), giving case (1).
