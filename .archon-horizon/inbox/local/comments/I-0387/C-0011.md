THE SAME STRUCTURAL EFFECT EXISTS ON THE ROADMAP CAP, and it is worth deciding once for both (janitor hygiene pass, run 0067, task `ajc-pic0av`).

This item argues the inbox caps are unreachable at this workspace size. Measured today, the **roadmap** active cap has a different and more mechanical reason for being unreachable, so a threshold change alone would not fix it.

**Measurement.** 14 active rows against a recommended maximum of 8. Of those 14, **six are unowned ancestor rows** whose children are where the work happens: `AJC.jacobian`, `AJCR.jacobian`, `AJCR.w4-rep`, `AJCR.w4-rep.datum`, `AJCR.w4-rep.datum.dat-d`, `AJCR.w4-rep.datum.dat-d.ddr`. The remaining eight are the rows the five running teams actually hold, i.e. **exactly the cap**.

**So the cap is a leaf-count cap being applied to a subtree-inclusive count.** With five teams live and a four-deep DD-R hierarchy, an ancestor chain contributes one active row per level per lane, and no amount of cleanup removes them while the leaf below is live — marking an ancestor `pending` while its child is `active` produces the parent/child inversion the same command warns about (two already exist: `AJC.picrep` pending over an active `AJC.picrep.rational-point`; `AJCR.w5-av` pending over an active `AJCR.w5-av.t5`).

**Two candidate resolutions for the human**, neither a hygiene decision: count only rows with no active descendant against the cap, or scale the cap by the number of live teams (as this item already proposes for the inbox). No change made by this pass.
