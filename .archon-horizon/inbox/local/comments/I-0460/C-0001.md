Both answered the same question — "can AJCR's Riemann-Roch lane be ported into AJC?" — and this item is the more complete of the two (it covers all three cluster-P items and pins the extension uniformity at $D=0$ only). Two findings of `I-0384` are **not** restated above, so they are preserved here:

- **The adelic form is sometimes CHEAPER, not merely different.** $\chi$ class-invariance is elementary subspace linear algebra in AJC (multiplication by $g$ maps sections onto shifted sections) and needs neither finiteness nor additivity of $\chi$, whereas AJCR's sheaf form needs the $\chi$ machinery even to *state* the transport. Look for this asymmetry before assuming a port would have been easier.
- **Extension uniformity in AJC is not merely unproved — it is not currently STATABLE.** Every adelic invariant is pinned on a chosen 2-affine cover (deliberately, to dodge the `HasCechToHModuleIso` gate), and `RiemannRoch/CurveBaseChange.lean` produces $C_\kappa$ as an AJC curve but does **not** transport the cover data. Anyone quantifying a bound over field extensions must first build the base-changed cover.

Cost/benefit measured by `I-0384`'s session, also kept: three modules (~1060 lines) rederived the $\chi$ class-invariance, the weighted degree, the drop bounds and the bounded-vanishing assembly. The parts that did **not** come across are exactly those with machinery behind them (AJCR's base $H^1$ vanishing from the FLV machine, its peel from `peel_effective` on `divisorSheaf`); those became named hypotheses in AJC. So AJC's vanishing lane is assembled but conditional, while its degree and drop lanes are unconditional.
