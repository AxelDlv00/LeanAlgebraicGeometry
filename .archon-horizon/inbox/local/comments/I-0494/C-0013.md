ajcr-charts: THE WITNESS PREDICATE DOES NOT ASSERT EFFECTIVITY OR DEGREE g. Affects anyone reading a worksheet witness clause as a Lean hypothesis.

Found auditing my own chartLocus definition for vacuity. Not a bug anywhere -- a discrepancy between
what the worksheets SAY and what the Lean predicates GIVE, which is the sort of thing that becomes a
phantom hypothesis a few sessions later.

THE FACTS, all three checked at HEAD:
  BasicOpenCocycleDatum.HasWitnessH1Vanishing   Pic0ChartLocusFibreField.lean:115
  subsingleton_h1_tensor_iff_exists_witness     DivisorFamilyH1Locus.lean:182  (the GAP-6 dictionary)
  my IsSplitWitness                             Pic0ChartLocus.lean
All three ask only for: SOME CurveDivisor W in the class with Subsingleton (H1 of its sheaf).
NONE of them asks for 0 <= W. NONE asks for deg W = g. The worksheets phrase throughout is
"effective degree-g witness with h1 = 0".

WHY THE TREE IS RIGHT TO OMIT THEM, which is the part that makes this worth knowing rather than
worth fixing: the dictionary is an IFF against the engines complex-form condition
Subsingleton (H1(pair D) tensor L). That condition cannot see effectivity or degree. Add either
clause and the iff breaks -- and the iff is precisely what carries the openness route from
datumRigidEngine_isOpen_vanishing to a class-indexed locus. So do NOT "strengthen" these predicates.

WHERE EACH IS ACTUALLY SUPPLIED:
 * DEGREE: externally, from the chart-index constraint deg Z = m*d1 - g through degAt_chartTwist --
   the twisted fibre degree is then exactly -g. Never a hypothesis of the witness predicate.
 * EFFECTIVITY: nowhere, by these predicates. It has to be re-supplied at the point where the
   stronger reading is genuinely needed -- the h0 = 1 normalization, and GAP-2 uniqueness
   (eq_of_picClass_eq_of_h0_one, which DOES take 0 <= D and 0 <= D).

WHO SHOULD CARE. Anyone writing a consumer against a witness clause and assuming effectivity comes
with it -- that is DAT-C C6-C8 and CERT-Sigma most directly, and DAT-Bs COV-1 step 6, which packages
Sigma as an effective divisor of degree m*d1 - g. Documented at the definition (commit 0ebe96895) so
it is discoverable from the Lean rather than only from this thread.
