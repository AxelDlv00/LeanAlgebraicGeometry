This item keeps the anti-pattern name; the comment below adds the second form.

## The second form: a bare `Equiv` in a reduction chain

A lane isolates the numerical content of a keystone as an explicit hypothesis — good discipline: no `sorry`, no staged fact, machine-checked dependency — and then states in prose that the keystone is "reduced to" whatever computes that hypothesis.

**The reduction is only valid if every link from the hypothesis's subject to the computed object transports the invariant in the hypothesis.** In the Wave-5 case the invariant is `finrank` and one link, $T_e X \simeq \ker(G(k[\varepsilon]) \to G(k))$, is a bijection of **sets**. `Equiv` preserves cardinality and nothing else; `finrank` needs at least an `AddEquiv` plus, across a residue-field change, the intertwining law. So the "reduction" was a re-partition, not a reduction.

**Why the file's own warning did not prevent it:** the warning sat inline on the declaration ("a bijection of sets: it does not transport `finrank`"), while the overstatement sat in the module docstring, the commit message and the roadmap comment. Nobody re-reads the declaration when planning the next session — they read the summary. Same failure geometry as this item's docstring cases.

## The two cheap checks

1. For a conditional theorem with hypothesis `H`, ask what **type** of object `H`'s two sides live over, then walk the chain link by link asking *does this link preserve that structure*. `Equiv` preserves cardinality; `AddEquiv` preserves additive structure; only a `LinearEquiv` (or an `AddEquiv` plus a compatible scalar law) preserves rank.
2. Before writing "reduced to" in a commit message or roadmap comment, check that the reduction survives being stated **without** the prose — i.e. that there is a Lean statement of the form `H → keystone` with nothing hand-waved between them.
