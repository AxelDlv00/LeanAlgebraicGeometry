ESSENTIAL COMPANION, added by the same session that wrote this note: the lesson above is only HALF the story, and taken alone it is dangerous advice.

This note says: when a gap claims missing machinery, re-read the quantifiers of the hypotheses you
already have, because one may be more generous than where you have been instantiating it. True,
and it closed the AJC chi-ledger.

WHAT IT OMITS. Widening your reading of one quantifier can make the hypothesis UNSATISFIABLE at
the new instances -- and the project may be able to prove the negation there. That is exactly what
happened here, found by an independent work-reviewer audit within the same session
(see [[I-0451]] and issue I-0449):

hbump : forall P E, chi(1.P+E) = chi(E) + residueDeg P. I correctly noticed the 'forall E' is
unrestricted and used it to telescope the negative part. I did NOT audit the OTHER quantifier,
'forall P'. The project's only producer of such an instance, chi_add_eq_residueDeg, requires
P.point in the cover overlap; and at primes OFF the overlap the section space does not change, so
the exact sequence forces a chi-jump of 0, contradicting the residueDeg P >= 1 that hbump asserts.
Now a theorem: Adelic.not_bump_of_notMem_overlap.

So the corrected rule is TWO-SIDED:
  1. When a gap says 'we lack machinery M', re-read your hypotheses' quantifiers -- you may
     already be entitled to more than you have used. (This note.)
  2. Having widened one, audit the OTHER quantifiers of the same hypothesis for instances where
     the project can DERIVE THE NEGATION. Consistency is not enough: a hypothesis can be
     consistent (mine is, vacuously) and still refutable at every instance that matters.

Neither #print axioms, nor sorry counts, nor elaboration probes see step 2 -- I ran all three and
they were clean. Only reading the PRODUCER's side conditions catches it. My conclusion survived
(the ledger really does follow from hbump), but my account of what hbump costs was false in five
docstrings, and I had DMed it to another team.
