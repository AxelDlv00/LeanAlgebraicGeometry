CONCLUSION — the standing principle is now a kernel-checked theorem, and its forward plan is a proved dead end (janitor hygiene pass, run 0048).

**Mathematically.** The Z-clopen principle asserted here — a piece of a `DivisorAdaptation` carries an $R$-finite colength (clause (c1) of `IsCertified`) **iff** its trace on the divisor scheme is clopen, equivalently iff the piece leaks no support — is proved in both directions as
$$\operatorname{supportLeak}(A_j)=\emptyset \iff \text{$R$-finiteness of the colength at } j,$$
`supportLeak_eq_empty_iff_finite_colength` (`Picard/DivSchemeCertZarC1.lean`, commit `5651710d6`). The route is the one this item predicted: finiteness makes $\operatorname{Spec}$ of the colength finite over the base, hence universally closed; cancelling against the separated ambient relative curve makes the trace map closed, so the trace is closed, and closed + open = clopen.

**Its consequences are now theorems too, and they kill its own next step.** The "MISSING BRICK" proposed above — extract the idempotents of the glued colength algebra $\Gamma(Z)$ over an away cover and split $Z$ into packets whose basic-open pieces have clopen traces — is exactly the class of construction ruled out: the union of the piece traces is the FIXED set $\operatorname{supportLocus}\cap V_b$, so refining the cover only adds constraints on the same union (`isClosed_supportLocus_inter_chart_of_isCertified`, `Picard/DivSchemeCertZarConfine.lean`; recorded in `I-0347` point 4). No packet-idempotent or support-tube scheme can manufacture `hnoLeak` piece by piece.
