\section*{Mathematical diagnosis}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The separably-closed representer is available, but the passage from its finite affine atlas to one universal object is not yet expressed as a single theorem.
\item The current work strengthens local restriction, overlap, and base-change identities. Those identities are necessary, but they do not by themselves prove that the glued carrier represents the functor.
\item The quotient step needs projectivity or affine-orbit control for the exact carrier that represents the functor. A theorem about another carrier cannot discharge this obligation.
\item Until the universal property and exact-carrier geometry are settled, the original-field representer and the Jacobian datum cannot be assembled from one representing object and one universal class.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{The route decision}
The central mathematical question is:
\begin{quote}
Is \code{P.gluedOver} genuinely the finite-level representing scheme required by the reviewed descent theorem, or is the current strategy constructing an atlas without yet identifying its universal property?
\end{quote}

If it is the correct carrier, the next meaningful milestone is one theorem saying that \code{P.gluedOver} represents the finite-stage Pic0 functor, including its universal class, naturality for every test scheme, and the compatibility needed for Galois descent. If that theorem cannot be stated without changing carriers or adding unrelated hypotheses, the route needs redesign.
