\section*{3. The two mathematical producer gaps}

\band{redbg}{redfg}{GAP A --- finite-stage Yoneda representability}
\textbf{Missing statement.} For every test scheme \code{T} over \code{Spec k}, construct a precomposition-natural equivalence between maps to \code{P.gluedOver} and \code{pic0TypeFunctor C T}. Equivalently, produce the following Lean proposition with the exact carrier and no additional binder:
\begin{verbatim}
(pic0TypeFunctor ((baseChange K P.N.1).obj C)).RepresentableBy P.gluedOver
\end{verbatim}

\textbf{Why the current objects do not prove it.} The glue package gives charts, overlaps, and a glued scheme. \code{finiteStageBaseChangeIso} and \code{gluingGluedIso} give an isomorphism of schemes after base change. Neither statement identifies maps from an arbitrary \code{T} with Pic0 families on \code{T}; that identification is the missing natural \code{homEquiv}. The roadmap names this target explicitly and records that no producer for it exists.

\band{amberbg}{amberfg}{GAP B --- geometry of the exact finite carrier}
\textbf{Missing statement.} Prove \code{FiniteInAffine P.glueData.glued}, or an equivalent finite-dimensional projective-space immersion/projectivity theorem, over the arbitrary finite Galois stage used by \code{P}. The statement must concern the same carrier that is supposed to represent the functor.

\textbf{What is conditional already.} \code{Pic0FiniteStageOrbitAffine.lean} has producers after consuming \code{rep}; some require an algebraically closed field plus connectedness or irreducibility, while another requires an explicit immersion or projectivity input. The stable-cover wrappers consume the same hypotheses. There is no unconditional arbitrary-field producer before Gap A. This is a genuine geometric gap unless the reviewed finite-type/proper/group facts imply the missing hypotheses.

\section*{5. Why the route keeps reaching the same boundary}

The current strategy accumulates restriction, overlap, diagram, and base-change
identities for a finite atlas. Those facts show that \code{P.gluedOver} is a
plausible model and that its scalar extension matches the separably-closed model.
They do not prove the universal assertion
\[
\mbox{for every }T,\quad \operatorname{Maps}(T, P.\mathrm{gluedOver})
\mbox{ is naturally }\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(T).
\]
That assertion is the first producer theorem consumed by every later descent
construction. The orbit-affine lemmas then consume the missing \code{rep}; they
cannot create it. Thus local identities can all be correct while the route still
stops at Gap A.

\section*{6. Mathematical conclusion}
\statusbox{redbg}{redfg}{The unresolved route is concentrated in two concrete claims: (A) finite-stage functor-level representability by \code{P.gluedOver}; and (B) exact-carrier orbit geometry over an arbitrary finite Galois stage. These are the places where a new mathematical idea, a stronger hypothesis, or a different carrier may be required.}

\statusbox{bluebg}{bluefg}{Class gluing, effective finite-Galois descent, and the Jacobian handoff are conditional downstream work. They should be attempted only after the two producer statements are mathematically justified, and they should preserve the same representing scheme and universal class.}

\noindent\textbf{The useful review decision is therefore concrete:} validate or reject Gap A and Gap B as stated. If either theorem cannot be formulated for the exact carrier without extra hypotheses, the present route needs redesign; further local overlap identities will not resolve that issue.
